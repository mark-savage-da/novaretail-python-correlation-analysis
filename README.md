# NovaRetail Python Correlation Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer behavior at **NovaRetail+**, an e-commerce platform operating in Latin America.

The objective is to identify which customer behavior variables are most strongly associated with the annual revenue generated by each customer.

The analysis follows an exploratory correlation approach and focuses on identifying relevant patterns without assuming causality.

## Business Question

**Which customer behavior factors are most strongly associated with annual revenue?**

The analysis supports the Growth and Retention team by identifying variables that may help explain differences in customer value.

## Dataset

The project uses a customer behavior dataset containing **15,000 records** and variables related to:

- customer age
- estimated income level
- monthly visits
- monthly purchases
- targeted advertising spend
- customer satisfaction
- premium membership
- customer churn
- device type
- region
- annual revenue

The main target variable is:

`ingreso_anual`

## Key Findings

### 1. Monthly purchases show the strongest relationship with annual revenue

`compras_mes` presents the strongest positive association with `ingreso_anual`.

This suggests that purchase frequency is the behavioral variable most closely associated with customer economic value in this dataset.

### 2. Monthly visits provide a moderate engagement signal

`visitas_mes` shows a positive but weaker relationship with annual revenue.

Higher platform activity may indicate customer interest or engagement, but visits alone do not necessarily translate into purchases.

### 3. Advertising spend and categorical variables show weaker relationships

`gasto_publicidad_dirigida` shows a weaker positive association with annual revenue.

The categorical analysis also suggests that variables such as region and device type do not show strong associations in this dataset.

## Important Analytical Consideration

**Correlation does not imply causation.**

The results identify statistical associations between variables, but they do not demonstrate that one variable directly causes changes in another.

Additional experimentation, segmentation, and predictive analysis would be required to evaluate causal relationships.

## Next Steps
